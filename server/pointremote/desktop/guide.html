PointRemote guide

Point your phone at a light or air conditioner and it switches. This guide gets you from nothing to your first switch in about half an hour, most of it spent taking photos.

  1. Install the phone app
  2. Let the phone reach this PC
  3. Connect the phone
  4. Connect Home Assistant
  5. Photograph your devices
  6. Train
  7. Point and switch
  8. Add the widget

1Install the phone app

On your Android phone (Android 10 or newer), open the latest release and download PointRemote.apk. Open it from your downloads.

Android will ask whether to allow installing apps from your browser. Allow it for this install. You can switch it off again afterwards.

2Let the phone reach this PC

The phone and the PC must be on the same Wi-Fi or home network.

  • Windows: the first time the server starts, Windows asks whether to allow it on networks. Tick Private networks and click Allow.
  • Mac: if macOS asks whether to accept incoming connections, click Allow.
  • Linux: if you use a firewall, open port 8765 for your home network, for example with ufw: 
    sudo ufw allow from 192.168.0.0/16 to any port 8765 proto tcp

Check from the phone: open the address shown in the PointRemote window (like http://192.168.1.20:8765/ui) in the phone's browser. If a login page appears, you're set.

3Connect the phone

In the app, open Settings, then under PC training server tap Scan QR from PC, and point the camera at the QR code in the PointRemote window. The address and access key fill in by themselves. Tap Test to check.

The window's "Phone last connected" line turns green once the phone has talked to the PC.

The QR code contains your access key. Don't post a screenshot of it.

4Connect Home Assistant

  1. In Home Assistant, click your name at the bottom left, open the Security tab, and under Long-lived access tokens click Create token. Name it PointRemote and copy it.
  2. In the app's Settings, under Home Assistant, enter your Home Assistant address (like http://192.168.1.10:8123) and paste the token.
  3. Tap Test. It should say how many devices it found.

Getting the token to your phone is easiest by creating it in Home Assistant's own app or web page on the phone.

5Photograph your devices

Open Capture. It asks what you're photographing every time, so photos never land in the wrong place.

  1. Tap + Add a device from Home Assistant and pick a light, fan, switch or air conditioner. Search helps if the list is long.
  2. Hold the round shutter button and walk slowly around the device. It takes about three photos a second. Aim for about 100 photos: near and far, from the sides, with the light on and off, by day and at night.
  3. Now pick Unknown and photograph everything that isn't a device: walls, ceiling, furniture, the floor, other rooms. Take at least as many as for your devices. This is what stops the app from switching things when you're just waving the phone around.
Start with two or three devices. Add more later, a few at a time.

6Train

Open Train and tap Train. The PC learns your devices from the photos. With an NVIDIA graphics card that takes a few minutes; without one, expect ten to thirty.

When it finishes, the screen shows how accurate it is overall and per device, and which devices it mixes up. The phone downloads the new model by itself.

You can also look at your photos and delete bad ones from the PC: click Photos and training in the PointRemote window.

7Point and switch

Open Point and aim at a device. Once it's sure (several frames in a row agree), the name locks in. Tap to switch it.

8Add the widget

Long-press your home screen, choose Widgets, and add PointRemote. Tapping it opens a bare camera that switches the first device it recognises, then closes. There's also a Quick Settings tile.

If you'd rather confirm every switch, turn off Switch without tapping in Settings.

9If something doesn't work

  • Phone can't reach the PC. Check that both are on the same network, and turn off any VPN on the phone; VPNs send the traffic elsewhere. Then check the firewall step again.
  • It mixes up two devices. Take more photos of both from the angles where they look alike, then train again.
  • It reacts to things that aren't devices. Take more Unknown photos of those things, then train again.
  • It doesn't recognise a device you just added. Train again. The model only knows the photos it was trained on.
  • The server window shows an error. Click Try again. If it keeps failing, the details are in the log file in the PointRemote data folder (logs/server.log).
PointRemote talks over plain HTTP and is meant for your home network only. Don't open port 8765 to the internet.
